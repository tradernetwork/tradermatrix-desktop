Market posture lens (reference card, version 1). It defines what market posture means and says how little of it a single-symbol digest can show. It describes; it does not tell the user how exposed to be.

Words
- Market posture: a summary of the market's backdrop used to frame risk: how many stocks are taking part in moves (breadth), what kind of regime the market is in (broadening, concentrated, contracting), how stretched or stressed it looks, and how much capital the backdrop appears to support. It is a frame for a conversation, not a trade.
- Breadth: the share of stocks rising, above their moving averages or making new highs. Narrow breadth means a few names carry an index.
- Regime: a label for the market's recent character, for example trending, range-bound or stressed. A label summarizes the past; it does not predict.
- Exposure: how much of the account is committed to the market. An exposure ceiling is the most a framework allows in a given backdrop. Only the user decides what to hold.
- Distribution day and follow-through day: index sessions of heavy selling or a strong rebound that some frameworks count to judge a turn. They are counts, not triggers.

What this digest does show (one symbol, not the market)
- IV rank: where this symbol's implied volatility sits in its own past year (0 to 100). It is not a market-wide fear gauge.
- LEVELS em_pct: the options-implied expected move for this symbol, as a decimal fraction of price.
- LEVELS flip: this symbol's dealer gamma flip, a positioning map.
- RANGE chg: this symbol's change over the last 30 bars; VOL est_buy_share: an estimate from bar closes, not tape data.

What the data cannot tell you
- Index trend, breadth, sector flow, regime, distribution counts, macro data or the user's total exposure. None are in the digest. If asked about them, say they are not available here (NA) and name what is missing.
- A single symbol's numbers do not describe the market. Do not stretch them into a market call.
- Never say how much to hold, whether to add or cut exposure, or whether the backdrop is safe. Describe what the numbers show and what is missing.
